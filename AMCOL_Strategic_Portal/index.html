What is a fenceline community?

In Trinidad & Tobago's industrial and energy sectors, a fenceline community generally refers to a community located in close proximity to a company's major installations or operating activities. The National Gas Company of Trinidad and Tobago (NGC), for example, defines its local or fenceline communities according to their proximity to major installations and operations.

These communities can have direct social and economic connections to nearby industrial activity through employment, contracting, services, community programmes and other forms of local participation.

AMCOL's role in the local industrial ecosystem

AMCOL's ability to respond to industrial demand is supported by a wider local network that includes employees, warehousing, logistics providers, contractors, technical resources and service partners. A stronger Massy Wood × AMCOL relationship can therefore create value beyond the immediate supply of products.

As local procurement grows, it can help support:

  • Local employment, experience and skills development.
  • Opportunities for Trinidad & Tobago contractors and service providers.
  • Continued development of local warehousing, logistics and technical capability.
  • Greater participation by local businesses in industrial projects.
  • More industrial expenditure circulating through the domestic economy.
  • Stronger locally available stock and response capability.

Strengthening local participation

The principle is practical: local content and local supply can help retain more economic value within Trinidad & Tobago while improving access to the products and services needed by active industrial work-fronts.

T&T 2030 · AMCOL solar solutions · Massy Wood action

From Renewable Target to Practical Deployment

01 · The policy foundation

The Paris Agreement provides the global framework for climate action. It aims to hold the increase in global average temperature to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels while pursuing efforts to limit warming to 1.5°C. Countries implement the Agreement through Nationally Determined Contributions, or NDCs, which are submitted in recurring five-year cycles.

02 · Trinidad & Tobago's 2030 renewable target

At COP26 in 2021, Trinidad & Tobago committed to target 30% of its power generation from renewable energy by 2030. The country's Second NDC retains this as a conditional commitment and states that the target is expected to be pursued through solar photovoltaic and wind generation.

National 2030 target 30% share of power generation from renewable energy by 2030, conditional on international support. Trinidad & Tobago's NDC estimates that achieving this renewable-power target would result in an additional reduction of approximately 17 million tonnes CO2-equivalent by 2030.

03 · The implementation period

Trinidad & Tobago's Second NDC covers the 2025–2035 period, with an interim target in 2030. The transition is therefore not a single Caribbean-wide CO2 Act that suddenly applies to every business on one date. It is a phased national programme involving renewable-generation capacity, grid development, policy and legislation, financing, technology and private-sector participation.

The NDC also notes that small-scale solar photovoltaic installations are already being implemented nationwide and are expected to increase, while government policy intends to support further renewable-electricity participation by individuals and businesses.

04 · What AMCOL can support today

The AMCOL connection is not theoretical. Current AMCOL offerings and published project activity provide concrete examples of solar-related capability:

  • Wagan Tech Solar + LED Floodlight 1600: a solar-powered outdoor/security floodlight currently listed by AMCOL Hardware.
  • Wagan Tech Solar + LED Floodlight 3000: a higher-output solar-powered outdoor light currently listed by AMCOL Hardware.
  • Solar-project supply support: AMCOL Industrial reports supplying anchors, panel-mounting supports and consumables for a grid-tied utility-scale solar installation in Central Trinidad.

These examples establish a defensible starting point: AMCOL can supply certain solar-powered products and has demonstrated supply support for solar-installation work.

05 · How this can translate into Massy Wood action

Massy Wood can identify operational applications where a solar-powered alternative is technically suitable. Potential applications could include outdoor lighting, pathways, yards, parking areas, remote locations or other uses where the selected AMCOL product is appropriate for the required duty.

Where the solar solution replaces electricity that would otherwise come from the grid, a generator or another conventional energy source, it can reduce that conventional energy demand. The size of any emissions benefit depends on the actual application, operating hours, displaced energy source and system performance.

Recommended partnership model Identify application → establish current energy baseline → specify AMCOL-supported solar solution → deploy → measure energy displaced → calculate defensible emissions benefit → scale successful applications.

06 · How this relates to the 2030 mission

A Massy Wood purchase of an AMCOL solar product should not be described as automatically delivering a specific percentage of Trinidad & Tobago's national 30% renewable-power target. National accounting is broader and the 30% target concerns the share of power generation from renewable sources.

The stronger and more accurate message is that practical adoption of solar-powered technologies can support the same national direction: increasing renewable-energy use, reducing dependence on conventional power in suitable applications and building experience with lower-carbon technologies across industry.

For Massy Wood, that creates a practical sustainability pathway. For AMCOL, it creates an opportunity to support that pathway with real products, local supply capability and relevant project materials.

07 · Beyond 2030

Trinidad & Tobago's Second NDC also maintains a conditional commitment to reduce cumulative emissions from power generation, transportation and industry by 15% relative to business-as-usual by 2035. Renewable deployment is therefore part of a longer transition rather than a one-time 2030 exercise.

T&T 2030 TARGET → MASSY WOOD APPLICATION → AMCOL SOLAR SOLUTION → REDUCED CONVENTIONAL ENERGY USE → MEASURED BENEFIT → SCALE

The partnership message is simple: use the national 2030 direction as the strategic context, identify real Massy Wood applications, deploy suitable AMCOL solar solutions, and measure the result before making carbon-reduction claims.

Product availability and suitability should be confirmed by AMCOL before any project commitment.
